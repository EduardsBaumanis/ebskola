# Review · 12 · Student Perspective Reviewer

> Improvement plan from the student's point of view: difficulty, motivation,
> hidden prerequisites and confusion points.

Severity: `Critical | Major | Minor | Nit`.

## What I reviewed

- `datorika9/dat9_11.html`, `programmesana1/prog1_1/prog1_11.html`,
  `robotika/rbtk7_11.html` — read as a first-time student at each grade level.

## Findings

- **Positive** — Datorika 9 opens gently ("make your own browser game") and its
  first lesson is concrete: create a folder, save `11.html`, see it in the browser.
  A nervous beginner can follow it.
- **Minor · English UI labels** — lessons reference English interface strings
  (Start, New → Folder, Install, Get, Sign in, Open with → Edge). Translations are
  usually present, but the convention varies. For a student who doesn't know
  English, every label should be glossed the same way.
- **Minor · goal at the end** — "Gatavs, kad:" appears after the steps. Students
  benefit from knowing the target *before* they start; move or duplicate it up top.
- **Positive · fear management** — P1's "salauz kodu un iemācies lasīt kļūdu"
  task turns errors into learning, and Robotika's calibration guidance
  ("robots negriežas par 90° — maini skaitli") prevents frustration.
- **Verification item** — ensure each lesson states what the student will have
  made by the end (a file, a page, a program, a moving robot) in the first screen.

## Improvement plan

1. Standardize English-UI-label glossing across all lessons.
2. Put the success criterion (what "done" looks like) at the start of each task.
3. Add a one-line "Šajā stundā tu…" motivation at the top of every lesson.
4. Read each lesson once as a beginner and flag hidden prerequisites (a tool, a
   term, or a prior step not yet taught).
5. Check the troubleshooting ("Ja kaut kas neizdodas") answers the real first
   question a stuck student would ask.
